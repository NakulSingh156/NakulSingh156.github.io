Hello there! I want to share my whole Google Summer of Code (GSoC) 2026 experience with DBpedia in this post and also share my insights and tips for all the students who are targeting for GSoC 2027.
Let's take it a little back, it was September 2023, and it was my first year of college. I used to watch a lot of YouTube videos about the opportunities I should be targeting for as an engineering student. And that is when I got to know about GSoC! I watched the video of Shradha Khapra ma'am and I was like, damn! This is huge. I researched more about it, watched more videos about how to actually get selected in GSoC. 
I saw the official website of GSoC, I saw the list of contributors and their projects in the GSoC 2022 program.
And that is when I promised to myself, that my name will also be here someday.

It was a random day in January 2026, 7th January to be precise, evening time and I was just sitting in my hostel room staring at the screen of my mac. I was looking at a project of mine, and I was trying to thinking of the ways I can improve it. And then a thought randomly popped in my head, wait isn't this January?! GSoC should be right around corner!! 
This literally came out of no where! And I immediately opened a new tab in my browser and opened the GSoC website and saw the timeline. I got to know that contributors have to submit their proposals in March, and the program starts in May. So from January to March, contributors have to contribute to the projects of any organisations of their choice and interact with the mentors of that project. 
And I realised, if I start contributing now then by March I should be having good enough contributions to get selected.

One of the most important things for GSoC selection is starting early, so that you can do enough work that's required to take you through, and the mentors of the organisation you selected can get familiar with you and start recognising you.
So the next step for me was to pick the correct organisation, and that's when DBpedia came into picture. While I found the projects of other organisations to be very hard and complex, I was able to understand what DBpedia actually do and I got really interested in their Neural Extraction project. I dug inside that project and went to its core, and that's when I realised and said to myself, this is the project I'm going to work on! It matched my desired tech stack: AI/ML, Data Science and Python and I genuinely was able to understand the project and was trying to map out solutions I can build to improve it.

And I locked in DBpedia and now it was time to go all in! I contributed for 2 months, made meaningful PRs and interacted with the mentors at the same time. And on 22nd March I submitted my 37 page GSoC proposal. It had the full details of my PRs, a prototype I built as a proof of work and my ideas for the summer, of how can I improve the project. I literally gave everything I had in these 3 months, every day of mine was just focussed around this. I literally couldn't recognise myself, because the work I did in these 3 months literally transformed me a lot! There were so many new things I learnt on the way.

And now it was a game of patience, results were to be announced on 1st May, and till then its suspense. I kept overthinking about my chances, even doubting my own work. But at the same time there was a sense of calmness and assurance in my mind at the same time that I gave my absolute everything, I left no stone unturned and even if I didn't get selected, the work that I did for this project till now and the skills I learnt, no one can take that away from me. 
Days kept passing and it finally was the night of 30th April 2026, results were to be announced at 11:30 pm. And throughout they entire day I just kept thinking about this. I remember, when it was 11:20 pm, from then I started refreshing my inbox and the GSoC portal. It finally was 11:30, nothing popped up. It was 11:35, still nothing. I was kinda worrtied that maybe I'm not selected.

And then came a mail on 11:37 pm, "GSoC 2026: Congratulations, your proposal with DBpedia has been accepted!" 
My goodness!! My reaction was surprisingly very calm, that yeah I deserve it. You know through out those days I just kept thinking if I get selected then how would I celebrate. But when the moment actually came, I was surprisingly very calm. I told my roommate about this, we both celebrated together. Our third roommate was half asleep, we woke him and told about this, and then all three of us celebrated and danced in the room! Then I called my parents to tell about this, they were also very keenly waiting for this. They were happier than me! 

I received a very warm welcome from DBpedia community and my project mentors, we did a few introduction calls and I couldn't wait to get started with the coding. The time came, 1st June 2026, we kicked off the project. I maintained the same sincerity here as well. I won't get too much into the technical details of the project in this blog post, you can check out my weekly blogs where I've shared the progress of my project from start to end in depth. The things I loved about my mentors: Tommaso Soru, Ara Yeroyan, Nandana and Mayank, was how patient and calm they were with me. In the beginning of the project, it wasn't that easy and it was hard for me to figure out a few things. And they never made me feel dumb, they always supported me and actually "mentored" me. The thing I loved the most is that they won't spoon feed you, absolutely no hand holding, they will guide you and show you the direction and the rest you have to figure out yourself, use your own brain.

I adapted very quickly, started working on the project and always tried to find ways to improve it. And by the end of the project, the results spoke for themselves! I beat the score of this project's last year's version with my model, and that too 3-4 times cheaper and faster computation. Better performance in all the metrics. This was the result of 3 months of consistency, my desire to keep improving and finding my own bugs and fixing them. Then I packaged everything and made it a ready to use model using Docker. 
I passed the final submission, so grateful and thankful to my mentors. I mean I surely wouldn't be here without their guidance. And there are a few things that I would still be working on even after GSoC ends. Publishing the results and my full model in a research paper, and still keep on contributing to this project as a contributor. Open Source doesn't just end with GSoC final evaluation, the work should go on!

After GSoC completion, I finally found myself relaxing a little, with a lot of gratitude, that I got this opportunity and I was able to make the best of it. The things and skills I learnt this summer are really the things I value the most, and I know wouldn't be able to learn that much had I not been doing this GSoC project. Still a long way to go, but this really was a very important step in my journey, something I'll always be proud about. Because very few people in the world can claim this, "I'm a GSoC Contributor", and I'm so grateful to God that I am one of them.

Now this was my whole journey and experience of GSoC with DBpedia, a huge thanks to DBpedia for trusting me with this project and handing me this opportunity, I really hope I was able to add some value to this, and I will always continue to be a part of this project!

Now in the end I want to leave some advice for the students who are aiming for GSoC 2027, with the little experience I have and all the learnings I've had along the way.
